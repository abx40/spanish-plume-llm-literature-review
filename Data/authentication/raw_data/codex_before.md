**Status:** reproducible partial systematic search; completeness cannot be certified because Google Scholar returned a CAPTCHA before displaying results and the available Web of Science session had no Core Collection entitlement.

## Bottom line

- The two requested sources that were fully searchable produced **88 unique records**: 73 from Wiley/Royal Meteorological Society and 15 from AMS Journals Online. There was no DOI/title overlap between them.
- One Wiley result was a November 2023 complete-issue shell, leaving **87 records through the exact day cutoff**.
- Under the strict rule excluding any study that actually analyses non-European weather, the requested-source records yielded **29 verified eligible papers**, **31 other exclusions**, and **27 unresolved Wiley records** whose full text or article type could not be checked. Counting the post-cutoff shell, exclusions from the original 88 total **32**.
- An independent publisher/repository full-text cross-check found **13 additional eligible papers** that cannot honestly be attributed to Google Scholar or Web of Science because neither requested results page was available.
- The table below therefore contains **42 independently verified eligible papers**, but it is **not a defensible claim that these are all papers in the four requested databases**. Twenty-seven Wiley hits, three additional paywalled publisher candidates, the unobserved Google Scholar set, and the unobserved Web of Science Core Collection set remain completion gaps.

The Schultz et al. review and its dataset were not used to identify, screen, or count any record reported here. Every inclusion is supported by a direct database hit or independently located publisher/repository full text.

## Eligibility and screening rules

The literal search used adjacent words `Spanish plume`, case-insensitive, in the title, abstract, keywords, or article body. A paper was excluded when the only occurrence was in its reference list. Included records had to be English journal articles in meteorology, atmospheric science, climatology, or hydrometeorological hazard research; peer-reviewed article status was checked from publisher/repository article type and journal policy, including the [AMS peer-review policy](https://www.ametsoc.org/ams/publications/author-information/peer-review-information/) and the relevant RMetS journal author guidance. Books, issue shells, editorials, letters, news, profiles, tutorials, preprints, theses, and journal material without article-level peer-review evidence were excluded.

The geographic rule was applied strictly to the analysed data/cases: literature comparisons outside Europe did not by themselves disqualify a European study, but a paper that analysed an Australian, US, Oklahoma, or global weather dataset/case was excluded. The first citable online Version-of-Record date controlled the cutoff; this retains Hayward et al. (2023), first published online 30 May 2023 despite assignment to a September issue.

## Supplemental checks and exclusions

The independent full-text pass also excluded Carlson and Ludlam (1968), [10.3402/tellusa.v20i2.10002](https://doi.org/10.3402/tellusa.v20i2.10002), because it explicitly analyses severe-storm cases over both southern England/Belgium and the midwestern United States. Final published papers with zero occurrences of the adjacent phrase included Rigo et al. (2019), Piper et al. (2016), Mohr et al. (2020), and Morgenstern et al. (2023). Fluck et al. (2021) contained the phrase in a discussion/preprint version but not in the final published PDF. Other exclusions included a storm-top “Spanish” plume with interrupted/non-equivalent wording, tutorials, theses, articles after the cutoff, and International Journal of Meteorology items without article-level evidence that peer review had occurred.

## What is needed to close the review

A complete four-database answer requires (1) a Google Scholar results export or a user-authorised CAPTCHA session, (2) a Web of Science Core Collection search/export from an entitled account, and (3) lawful full text for the unresolved candidates above. Until those are supplied, the reported **42 verified papers are a lower bound, not an exhaustive corpus**.
