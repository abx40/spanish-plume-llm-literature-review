# Systematic search for meteorological journal articles mentioning “Spanish plume”

**Eligibility cutoff:** published on or before 13 August 2023  
**Search run:** 13 August 2026  
**Language/topic:** English-language, peer-reviewed journal articles about meteorology and European weather  
**Phrase rule:** the literal two-word phrase *Spanish plume* (searches were case-insensitive; capitalization was not used as an exclusion)

## Result

The four database searches returned **288 records** in total. DOI/title normalization and manual checking removed **87 duplicate records**, leaving **201 unique records** for screening. After applying the stated criteria, **84 articles were eligible** and **117 unique records were excluded**.

This search was conducted independently from the four database outputs. No pre-existing Spanish-plume review or review dataset was used to find, add, or screen papers.

## Deduplication and screening

Deduplication used DOI first, followed by normalized title, year and author checks. Title normalization removed database tags (for example, `[HTML]`), case and punctuation differences, Unicode dash variants, and minor wording variants such as “UK” versus “United Kingdom.” Potential fuzzy matches were then checked manually.

Full text, publisher HTML, or an accessible PDF was searched to distinguish body-text mentions from reference-list-only matches. For 2023 records, the version-of-record date was checked against 13 August. Research in interdisciplinary journals was retained when its primary analysis was meteorological (for example, synoptic controls on floods or meteorologically generated coastal waves).

Primary exclusion reasons were: books and book chapters; theses, conference papers, reports, news and other non-peer-reviewed material; editorials, letters, issue front matter, Society news, *Weather* Images/Spotlights and similar non-research content; non-English texts; studies outside Europe (or mixed Europe/non-Europe studies that could not be separated); nonmeteorological uses of “plume” and oceanographic rather than meteorological studies; and records in which the phrase occurred only in the reference list. A record could meet more than one exclusion reason, so these categories are not additive.

Examples of reference-only exclusions were Jones and Macpherson (1997), Spengler et al. (2005), Charney and Fritsch (1999), Bryan and Fritsch (2000), Hannesen et al. (1998), and the Istanbul nowcasting paper by Tuncay and Ali (2020). The 2023 Fery and Faranda WCD record was a discussion preprint at the cutoff; its peer-reviewed version appeared in 2024 and was therefore excluded.
